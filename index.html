Films Around the World

Where Are Movies Produced?

Average annual TMDB-listed movie productions per million residents, 1980-2025.

Northern Europe stands out for its high recorded film-production intensity, particularly Iceland (72.56), Estonia (21.86) and Denmark (17.48) average annual movie credits per million residents. In contrast, the United States records a lower population-adjusted rate (9.17), despite having substantially more total movie credits. This demonstrates how adjusting for population reveals a different geographical pattern from comparing production volumes alone.

Source: TMDB movie production-country credits and World Bank population (SP.POP.TOTL). Released films, 1980-2025. A multi-country production contributes one credit to each credited country. Annual rates are averaged across years with valid population; missing values are not zero.

02 / MOVIE SUCCESS

What Makes a Movie Successful?

From genre representation to box-office performance and audience ratings, explore how different measures reveal different perspectives on movie success.

Which Movie Genres Have Dominated Each Decade?

Drama and Documentary dominate recorded movie production across the decades, while Horror has steadily gained representation. Compare how each genre's presence has shifted from the 1980s to 2020-2025.

Drama remains one of the most consistently represented genres, increasing from 20,394 recorded films in the 1980s to 75,878 in 2020-2025. Documentary also expanded substantially, reaching 67,331 films in the final period. Meanwhile, Horror's share of genre assignments increased from 3.9% to 7.0%, suggesting a shift in the relative composition of TMDB's recorded productions.

Source: TMDB, 1980-2025. Movies with multiple genres are counted once within each assigned genre. The final period covers six years rather than ten. Changes in TMDB coverage over time may influence the observed trends.

Does a Bigger Budget Mean Bigger Box Office Returns?

Do higher production budgets lead to greater box-office returns? Explore the relationship between production spending and worldwide revenue across 14,939 films released between 1980 and 2025. Each point represents one movie, with logarithmic scales revealing financial differences across productions of all sizes.

Click a genre in the legend to highlight its movies. Hold Shift while clicking to select multiple genres. Hover over a point to view movie details.

Higher production budgets are generally associated with greater worldwide box-office revenue, although outcomes vary considerably between films. Some lower-budget productions generate revenue far exceeding their reported production costs, while others earn comparatively little despite substantial investment.

Source: TMDB, 1980-2025. Only films with reported positive budgets and revenues are included. Both axes use logarithmic scales. Worldwide box-office revenue is not equivalent to studio profit, as distribution, marketing and other costs are excluded.

Which Movie Genres Deliver the Highest Returns?

Compare median return on investment (ROI) across genres and explore how their rankings change between decades.

Select a release period to compare genre rankings. Hover over a bar or gold marker to explore its financial statistics. Gold markers indicate a median ROI of exactly 0%.

Across 1980-2025, Family records the highest median reported ROI (+74.27%), followed by Adventure (+66.84%) and Action (+57.59%). Western is the only genre with a negative overall median ROI (-33.05%), highlighting differences in recorded financial outcomes between genres.

Source: TMDB, 1980-2025. ROI is calculated as (worldwide revenue - production budget) / production budget x 100. Only films with positive reported budgets and revenues are included. Multi-genre films contribute once to each assigned genre. Genre-period combinations with fewer than 30 eligible movies are excluded. The 2020s period covers 2020-2025. Reported ROI is not equivalent to actual studio profit.

Do IMDb and TMDB Audiences Rate Genres Differently?

Compare mean ratings across 19 movie genres using the same set of films on both platforms. The distance between each pair of dots reveals the difference in their recorded ratings.

Hover over a dot or connecting line to compare IMDb and TMDB ratings and view the number of eligible movies in each genre.

TMDB records higher average ratings than IMDb for 17 of the 19 genres. Family shows the largest difference, with TMDB averaging 6.59 compared with IMDb's 6.14 (0.45-point gap). Horror also shows a notable difference (0.38 points). Documentary is an exception with IMDb averaging 0.14 points higher than TMDB.

Source: IMDb and TMDB, 1980-2025. Ratings are measured on a 0-10 scale, with the displayed axis narrowed to 5-8 to emphasise differences. Only movies with valid ratings and at least 100 votes on both platforms are included. Multi-genre movies contribute once to each assigned genre. Each genre's mean is calculated using the same eligible movies on both platforms.

How Have Leading Production Companies Changed Rank?

Explore how rankings of the ten most prolific production companies changed between 2000 and 2025. Companies are selected by total credited movies across the full period, then ranked within this group for each interval.

BBC held first place among the selected companies for the first three periods, before ARTE took the lead in 2015-2019 and retained it in 2020-2025. France Télévisions made the largest ranking gain, rising from 10th to 2nd, while Lifetime climbed from 9th to 3rd. These changes reflect shifts in TMDB's recorded production-company credits, rather than necessarily changes in industry-wide production dominance.

Source: TMDB production-company credits, 2000-2025. Rankings are among the ten selected companies, not all companies in the dataset. The final interval (2020-2025) covers six years; preceding intervals cover five. Each movie is counted once for every credited production company. Changes in TMDB coverage may influence the observed rankings.

04 / AUSTRALIA

Australia's Screen Production Landscape

Explore reported production expenditure across Australian states and compare Australia's genre representation with global filmmaking.

Where Is Australia's Screen Production Expenditure Concentrated?

Explore how reported screen production expenditure is distributed across Australian states. Larger circles represent greater expenditure, highlighting differences in production activity between states.

Hover over a circle to view the state's expenditure, financial year and share of the published national total.

In 2024/25, Victoria recorded the highest reported screen production expenditure among the four individually reported states, at $168 million (44.3% of the national total). New South Wales followed with $90 million (23.7%), while Queensland recorded $82 million (21.6%) and South Australia $23 million (6.1%). Together, these four states accounted for approximately 95.8% of the published national total, highlighting the concentration of reported expenditure in Victoria, New South Wales and Queensland.

Source: Screen Australia, Spend by Location, 2024/25. Expenditure is reported in AUD millions. Only NSW, QLD, VIC and SA are individually mapped. The remaining expenditure is reported under Other* and is not assigned to individual geographic locations. Figures are rounded as published. Circle areas are proportional to expenditure.

Australian vs Global Movie Genres

How does Australia's genre representation compare with global filmmaking? Compare the percentage of movies associated with each genre, with Australian productions on the left and global productions on the right.

Hover over a bar to view the genre's movie count and percentage of eligible productions.

Drama is the most represented genre in both Australian (34.9%) and global (32.3%) productions, with Australia recording a 2.6 percentage-point higher share. Thriller (10.6% versus 7.4%) and Horror (10.9% versus 9.0%) are also more strongly represented in Australia's recorded productions. In contrast, Music has a lower share in Australia (5.2%) than globally (7.9%). These differences suggest that Australia's recorded genre composition broadly follows global patterns, while placing relatively greater emphasis on certain dramatic and suspense-oriented genres.

Source: TMDB, 1980-2025. Percentages represent the share of genre-classified movies associated with each genre. Movies with multiple genres contribute once to each assigned genre, so percentages do not sum to 100%. Australia is defined by production-country credit and is also included in the global total. Results reflect TMDB's recorded coverage rather than a complete census of film production.
